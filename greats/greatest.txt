# Andrew Wiles. Fermat's Last Theorem stood open for over 350 years. It was the archetypal "probably impossible" problem. The general mathematical consensus was that it would never be solved, or at least not in our lifetimes. Wiles worked in complete secrecy for seven years, told nobody, and dropped the result at a 1993 lecture in Cambridge with zero prior warning. The audience and the entire math world were stunned. Even within the number theory community nobody had a clue he was working on it because he published nothing related during that period. Nobody expected it. Everyone thought it was impossible. That is Wiles.# Grigori Perelman. The Poincare conjecture stood open for nearly 100 years, was a Clay Millennium Problem, and was widely considered the hardest of the seven. Perelman had essentially disappeared from mathematics. He returned to Russia, lived quietly with his mother, published nothing for years, and most people assumed he was done with math. Then in 2002-2003 he posted three preprints on arXiv with no affiliation, no announcement, no fanfare. Nobody expected anything from him specifically. Nobody even knew he was working on Ricci flow. He was not on anyone's shortlist. And he not only proved Poincare but the full geometrization conjecture, classifying all closed 3-manifolds. Then he declined the Fields Medal, declined the Clay million dollars, and disappeared again. Wiles shocked the world with secrecy. Perelman shocked the world by coming out of nowhere after being presumed finished.# Ngo Bao Chau. The fundamental lemma was considered a dead-end wall. It was an identity of orbital integrals that Langlands proposed in the 1980s, and it was understood to be the single biggest obstruction to stabilizing the Arthur-Selberg trace formula, which is the engine of the entire Langlands program. People had been grinding on it for over 20 years with essentially zero progress. It was widely assumed it would never be proved, and the trace formula would remain stuck. Ngo's solution came from a direction nobody imagined. He converted an intractable problem in harmonic analysis into pure geometry using Hitchin fibrations, perverse sheaves, and support theorems. Nobody was looking at Hitchin fibrations in connection with the fundamental lemma. The entire approach was a bolt from nowhere. He turned a wall into a door and unlocked major parts of the Langlands program in one stroke. Fields Medal followed in 2010.
